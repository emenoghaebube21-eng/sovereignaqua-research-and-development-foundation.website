From Research to Measurable Community Impact

SovereignAqua's impact framework connects water stewardship, sustainable aquaculture and fisheries, food security, environmental responsibility, education, youth participation and livelihood development. The framework is designed to move from evidence and consultation to validated implementation and documented learning.

SOURCE-BASED PROGRAMME REFERENCE

Sustainable Aquaculture, Fisheries & Food Security Initiative

This page incorporates the programme architecture contained in the supplied GEF Small Grants Programme — CSO Challenge Program concept-note template. The website presents it as a proposed/developing initiative; it does not represent the concept note as an awarded grant, confirmed funding, or confirmed institutional partnership.

Project concept

The supplied concept proposes a 24-month initiative in Nigeria focused on sustainable aquaculture and fisheries development, food security, livelihood diversification, practical training, responsible water management and environmentally sustainable fish production.

The stated proposed site is Ebe-Igbu community, OYI Local Government Area, Awkuzu, Anambra State, Nigeria. The concept identifies a project budget ceiling of USD 300,000 in the template and records a preliminary budget of USD 250,000.

STATUS: PROPOSED / DEVELOPING

The four operating components

  • Sustainable Aquaculture Production — community-based fish production, site development, stocking, monitoring, water and fish-health management.
  • Skills, Youth & Community Capacity — practical training in production, handling, feed, water-quality awareness, processing, records and enterprise management.
  • Food Security, Livelihoods & Value Chains — harvesting, processing, storage, market access and diversified livelihood opportunities.
  • Environmental Management, Knowledge & Replication — water, wastewater, waste, biodiversity, climate resilience and documented lessons.
THEORY OF CHANGE

Equip communities → strengthen production → improve resilience

The supplied concept note states a simple theory of change: if communities receive sustainable aquaculture systems, practical training, improved production and processing practices, and responsible water-management approaches, then food security, livelihoods and resilience can improve because local sustainable fish production and income opportunities are strengthened.

01

Build capacity

Train youth and community participants in aquaculture, fish handling, water awareness, processing, storage, record keeping and enterprise practices.

02

Improve systems

Use environmentally responsible production, water management, fish-health practices, processing and value-chain methods.

03

Document results

Measure outputs and outcomes, record limitations and lessons, and develop knowledge products that can support responsible replication.

IMPACT DIMENSIONS

What the programme is designed to influence

Food Security

More reliable access to nutritious fish and stronger local aquatic-food production systems.

Livelihoods

Practical skills, value-chain participation and diversified income opportunities for participating households and community members.

Youth & Women

Participation in training, decision-making, production, processing, marketing and community environmental initiatives.

Water Stewardship

Responsible water use, water-quality awareness and appropriate wastewater and waste-management practices.

Environmental Resilience

Biodiversity safeguards, responsible stocking, biosecurity and climate-resilient planning.

Knowledge Replication

Documented methods, lessons and knowledge products that can be evaluated for adaptation in other communities.

SAFEGUARDS

The questionnaire becomes part of the implementation discipline

The supplied environmental and social screening identifies areas requiring continued management rather than treating risk as an afterthought. The website therefore incorporates a safeguards gate into the public programme architecture.

Screening area identified in conceptWebsite implementation principle
Community health and safetyTraining, supervision, safety procedures and appropriate protective measures before field activities.
Occupational health and safetyDefined work procedures, worker training, supervision and risk controls.
Pollution and wasteWater, wastewater and waste-management controls incorporated into production planning.
Resource efficiencyMonitor water, energy and material use and avoid unnecessary environmental pressure.
Climate vulnerabilityConsider flooding, drought, heat, rainfall and other climate/disaster risks in site and operational planning.
BiodiversityAvoid significant adverse biodiversity impacts and evaluate stocking, habitat and ecosystem risks.
Indigenous Peoples / customary interestsUse appropriate consultation and free, prior and informed consent processes where applicable.
Cultural heritageIdentify cultural, sacred or archaeological considerations before activities that could affect them.

Relevant state and federal agriculture/fisheries authorities for technical coordination, regulatory liaison and policy alignment.

Research & Universities

Potential technical and research collaboration with relevant academic departments and specialists.

Development & Knowledge Partners

Farmers, hatcheries, feed suppliers, processors, traders, markets and other actors needed to connect production to consumers.

NEXT CONTROL GATE

Validate before scaling.

Before the proposed initiative is presented as an active funded project, the Foundation should complete appropriate stakeholder consultation, site and technical validation, environmental and social review, detailed costing, regulatory review, implementation planning and confirmation of any funding or partnership status.
